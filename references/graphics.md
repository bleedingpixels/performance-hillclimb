# Optimize graphics with a visual-quality contract

Use this reference for rendering, XR, temporal aliasing, texture filtering, or a graphics optimization that changes fidelity. It turns the historical GearVR recipe supplied with the task into mechanisms to test. Its sample counts, old mobile-GPU defaults, shadow restrictions, and submission opinions are not current universal requirements. Primary API, renderer, and Unity documentation was checked on 2026-10-05; no headset measurements or rendering speedups were reproduced.

## Establish stable output before climbing

Aliasing includes false spatial detail and flicker during motion. A band-limited signal needs a sampling rate greater than twice its highest frequency for exact reconstruction; discontinuities are not band limited. Increasing one kind of sample does not necessarily sample every shader, texture, geometry, and temporal signal adequately. Filter the relevant footprint and test the reconstructed result. [PBRT sampling theory](https://www.pbr-book.org/4ed/Sampling_and_Reconstruction/Sampling_Theory)

Choose the requested outcome: less shimmer within a frame/resource budget, or lower cost while preserving an agreed quality floor. Inspect the active engine/version, graphics API, pipeline/path, shader variants, actual sampler/attachment state, stereo mode, refresh rate, render resolution, foveation, upscaler/temporal history, and reprojection/frame-synthesis mode. An Inspector checkbox is intent; it does not prove the active render path uses the feature.

Define a small representative sequence: slight head rotation and translation; static and moving subjects; near/far, oblique, high-contrast, thin, glossy, cutout, and UI regions. Preserve both eyes, scene/content identities, exact pose/time sequence, exposure, color transform, display settings, and cold/sustained state. Separate rendered-frame artifacts from compositor/display delivery; record metric units and observation intervals. A mirror screenshot cannot establish stereoscopic motion stability.

| Role | Useful evidence | Acceptance limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual outcome | Matched motion sequences and headset inspection of shimmer, moiré, crawling edges, popping, halos, and ghosting in named regions | Set the permissible change before tuning. Protect text readability, detail, material appearance, state freshness, and stereo consistency; blur or reduced contrast must not silently redefine quality. |
| Diagnostic | Mip/sampler/coverage/format captures; isolated geometry, diffuse, specular, shadow, and alpha passes; reference-image error maps | Explain the responsible signal. Static error, fewer triangles, or lower shader time is not a motion-quality win. |
| Temporal diagnostic | Residual error against a trusted sequence at matched poses, or motion-compensated stable regions | Label this as an exploratory metric. Account for parallax, legitimate view-dependent shading, motion, disocclusions, and alignment errors; raw frame differences can penalize correct motion. |
| Performance/resource | App CPU/GPU and compositor time, frame-delivery failures, consecutive misses, bandwidth/memory where observable, and sustained clocks/thermal state | Preserve the agreed budget and quality. `1000 / refresh_Hz` is a nominal interval, not a guaranteed app GPU allowance. Snapshot telemetry percentiles are not automatically individual-frame percentiles. |

Keep an independent output or high-quality reference and raw frames beside any edited demonstration video. A GIF, mirror capture, or one perceptual image score cannot prove headset quality. A faster lower-resolution candidate that violates readability or stability fails a performance optimization with those protected requirements. If quality is the requested outcome, accept improved quality only within its agreed resource contract.

## Diagnose the signal before choosing the knob

| Symptom or historical recommendation | Candidate experiment and modern boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Receding or oblique textures shimmer | Inspect complete, valid mip chains and effective minification filtering. Trilinear interpolates between filtered mip levels; anisotropy addresses elongated footprints and is complementary. Sweep a small supported range on target hardware. Positive LOD bias can smooth at the cost of detail; there is no universal bias or maximum-anisotropy winner. [OpenGL 4.6 §8.14](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/gl/glspec46.core.pdf), [anisotropic extension](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/EXT/EXT_texture_filter_anisotropic.txt) |
| Dynamically produced textures show old or crawling detail | Keep base level and required mips coherent before sampling. Measure generation, updates, synchronization, transfers, and full frame completion together. Regenerating mips may be a valid candidate, but every-frame generation is not a free universal rule; use valid reuse or bounded updates only when content and dependencies permit. Protect update freshness and mip completeness. |
| Silhouette edges need antialiasing | Compare the renderer's supported MSAA counts at the same resolution and quality contract. MSAA primarily improves primitive coverage; ordinary shading may reuse one shader result across samples. It is not a cure for texture/specular aliasing. Sample shading is a distinct feature and cost. Deferred+MSAA is technically possible, but engine support and G-buffer/lighting/resolve costs determine availability. [Sample shading](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/ARB/ARB_sample_shading.txt), [multisample textures](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/ARB/ARB_texture_multisample.txt) |
| Alpha edges, atlas leakage, or dark UI fringes | Inspect address modes, texture layout, mip gutters, RGB at transparent edges, and the alpha convention. Straight-alpha assets may need color dilation; premultiplied assets need matching preprocessing/filtering and blend factors. Premultiply in linear light before resampling when using that convention. Applying premultiplication after an already contaminated lookup does not generally repair it. Size gutters for the sampled mip/footprint range; eight pixels is not a guarantee, and padding does not replace correct atlas addressing. [Alpha interpolation](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#interpolation-alpha), [compositing](https://www.w3.org/TR/compositing-1/#simplealphacompositing) |
| Cutout leaves shimmer; blending cannot meet sorting requirements | Test renderer-supported alpha-to-coverage and coverage-preserving mip generation separately. The OpenGL control is `GL_SAMPLE_ALPHA_TO_COVERAGE`; Unity calls its ShaderLab control `AlphaToMask`. It intersects alpha-derived and geometric sample coverage; it cannot extend coverage outside the primitive or replace general translucent compositing. Desktop alpha-to-one is separate and absent from ES 3.2 core. Threshold discard can be intentional; check coverage, sorting, depth/stencil, derivatives, and side effects rather than banning every discard. [Multisample rules](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/ARB/ARB_multisample.txt), [ES 3.2 §§13.8, 15.1.1](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/es/3.2/es_spec_3.2.pdf), [AlphaToMask](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/SL-AlphaToMask.html) |
| Four samples are described as two bits of blending | Binary coverage with N equal-weight samples has N+1 covered-sample counts; four samples are not a literal two-bit framebuffer color limit. Eight-bit alpha also does not guarantee perfect filtering, blending, or display reconstruction. Separate coverage precision, color precision, and sampling. |
| Thin geometry or sharp seams crawl | Test screen-space footprint, distance-dependent representation, and attribute continuity. Bevels or alternative line representations can change appearance and cost. Duplicate vertices do not inherently alias when positions, attributes, and shader-visible behavior are equivalent; shared indices cannot repair discontinuous normals, UVs, or materials. This is an inference from interpolation semantics, not an index-sharing mandate. [GLSL 4.60 §4.5](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/gl/GLSLangSpec.4.60.pdf) |
| Normal-mapped specular flashes despite clean edges | Isolate the specular contribution; compare renderer-supported specular AA, distribution-aware prefiltering, or permitted normal-detail/roughness changes. Mipmapping then normalizing an averaged normal can lose the distribution needed for a stable highlight. Preserve material fidelity and measure extra cost. Filament has specular-AA controls; LEAN is a model/storage-dependent approach, not a drop-in shader rule. [Filament materials](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html), [LEAN authors' publication](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/papers/lean/) |
| Shadow edges crawl | Separate map sampling/projection, filtering, and temporal stability. Compare supported shadow resolution/filter/cascade policies or an authorized simpler contact/blob representation. PCF filters comparison results; averaging raw depths is different. Filterable moment methods have light-bleeding/precision tradeoffs. The old GearVR advice is not a ban on modern dynamic shadows. [PCF author explanation](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-ii-lighting-and-shadows/chapter-11-shadow-map-antialiasing), [variance shadow-map tradeoffs](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-8-summed-area-variance-shadow-maps) |
| Text/line art averages incorrectly | Audit color decoding, filter/mip generation, linear-light shading/blending, render targets/resolve, and final encoding. Color RGB and alpha/data channels need different treatment; linear HDR intermediates need not be sRGB formats. Encode display RGB at the intended boundary without double conversion. Desktop GL's sRGB framebuffer blending has explicit rules; GL 4.6 prefers prefilter decoding but permits postfilter conversion, so verify actual behavior. [Framebuffer sRGB](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/ARB/ARB_framebuffer_sRGB.txt), [GL 4.6 §8.24](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/gl/glspec46.core.pdf) |

These are alternatives to discriminate, not a request to change every setting. Preserve losing cohorts and isolate interactions: mip filtering, normal/specular filtering, coverage, blending, and color space can change one another's result. A temporal AA/upscaler candidate also needs moving-object, disocclusion, ghosting, and history-reset checks; it is not an automatic replacement for spatial filtering.

## Unity controls are version and pipeline specific

The following locations are checked against Unity 6000.0 / HDRP 17.0; inspect the installed version and active asset/camera before using them. No screenshots or platform settings were changed during this research.

- **Texture importer:** Generate Mipmap, Mipmap Filtering, Filter Mode = Trilinear, and Aniso Level are separate choices. Check global anisotropy overrides and streaming/mip limits. For cutouts, Preserve Coverage/Alpha Cutoff and Alpha is Transparency address different mip/edge problems. Enable sRGB for gamma-authored color RGB, not every data texture; normals use their normal-map import type. [Default texture importer](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/texture-type-default.html)
- **Project:** Player → Other Settings → Rendering → Color Space. Linear rendering still needs correct sRGB decoding for authored color images; it does not mean disabling their sRGB import flag. Audit the full material/output pipeline when migrating. [Color-space setting](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/set-project-color-space.html)
- **URP:** Active URP Asset → Quality → Anti Aliasing (MSAA), plus applicable camera/path support. Forward and Forward+ support MSAA; Deferred does not in this version. Camera AA options and MSAA have different compatibility constraints. A mobile Opaque Texture/StoreAndResolve limitation can make runtime MSAA differ from the setting. [URP AA](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/anti-aliasing.html), [path comparison](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/urp/rendering-paths-comparison.html)
- **HDRP:** MSAA requires forward Lit rendering, asset sample settings, and camera Frame Settings. Geometric Specular AA on Lit materials changes smoothness based on geometry; it is not a generic URP toggle or a proven cure for every normal map. Compare the material change within the agreed fidelity contract. [HDRP AA](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Anti-Aliasing.html), [geometric specular AA](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/Geometric-Specular-Anti-Aliasing.html)

## Match tools to proof

| Question | Artifact/tool | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is actually bound and drawn? | Unity Frame Debugger; supported RenderDoc/API captures; shader/source inspection | Inspect attachments, sample count, sampler/mips, formats, blend/depth state, and pass order. Paused/captured/replayed frames diagnose state; final timing needs ordinary runs. [Unity debugger](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/FrameDebugger.html), [RenderDoc source](https://github.com/baldurk/renderdoc) |
| Did a candidate preserve an image against a reference? | Matched raw frames; optional open-source LDR/HDR FLIP error maps | Specify exposure/tone mapping and viewing parameters. FLIP is an image-comparison diagnostic, not a temporal/stereo metric or a substitute for headset motion inspection. [FLIP implementation](https://github.com/NVlabs/flip) |
| Did the headset runtime deliver frames sustainably? | Runtime telemetry/HUD and CSV, e.g. OVR Metrics; supported OpenXR metrics | Fix resolution/foveation/refresh/frame-synthesis mode and thermal policy. App/TimeWarp GPU times may use microseconds; stale frames and synthesized display rate need runtime-specific interpretation. A capture is not sustained delivery evidence. [OVR definitions](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/ts-ovrstats/), [HUD/CSV](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/ts-ovrmetricstool/) |
| Is submission/pose usage invalid across runtimes? | OpenXR development validation and exact application frame/pose records | Prediction is not actual presentation. Available extensions/counters and their intervals are runtime dependent. The 2025 best-practices layer checks timing/composition usage; it does not measure aliasing and is a development tool. [Frame submission](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/OpenXR-Guide/blob/main/chapters/frame_submission.md), [validation-layer report](https://www.khronos.org/blog/new-openxr-validation-layer-helps-developers-build-robustly-portable-xr-applications) |

Verify local platform/API/capture support before installing or attaching tools. When capture is unavailable, inspect project settings/source, export accessible telemetry, and obtain matched frames/pose records through the application's existing instrumentation. Missing headset or compositor evidence narrows the claim; a desktop simulation remains a separate environment.

## A bounded first experiment

For a 4x-MSAA scene with clean rails but shimmering glossy floors, isolate diffuse versus normal/specular output at fixed resolution, pose, and actual MSAA state. Compare one supported specular-filtering candidate before increasing every sample count. Separately test cutout coverage rather than conflating the two causes. Confirm the winning candidate in an untuned scene and normal unprofiled sustained runs, retaining both-eye motion evidence, readability/material checks, and frame/resource results. Until those measurements exist, report a plan or hypothesis, not an accepted quality or performance gain.
